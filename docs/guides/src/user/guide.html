OnScreen v2.6

User Guide

Watching, listening and reading with OnScreen on the web, your TV and your phone

This guide is for the people who watch and listen. It covers signing in, finding things, the players, music, audiobooks, books, photos, live TV, downloads and your account, plus notes for each app.

Setting up the server, adding libraries and managing users is covered in the separate OnScreen Install & Administration Guide. When something in this guide depends on a server setting, it says ask your server's admin.

Covers OnScreen server v2.6 and the apps released with it: web, Android TV / Google TV, Fire TV, Android phone, Samsung (Tizen), LG (webOS), Xbox and Roku.

Screens in this guide come from a test server. Your server's libraries, titles and some buttons will differ.

1Welcome to OnScreen

1.1What OnScreen is

OnScreen is a media server. One computer keeps your household's movies, TV shows, anime, music, audiobooks, podcasts, books, comics and photos, and you watch, listen and read on your own devices.

The person who runs the server, your admin, decides which libraries you can open and can set limits on your account (for example a content-rating limit or how many streams you can play at once). Everything else in this guide you can do yourself.

Your progress follows you. Start a film on the TV, finish it on your laptop: every app reads the same watch history, resume points, favorites and ratings from the server.

1.2The apps and where to get them

All the apps talk to the same server. You need your server's address (for example https://media.example.com or 192.168.1.10:7070) and an account. Your admin gives you both.

1.3Signing in on the web

  1. Open your server's address in your browser.
  2. Enter your Username and Password, then click Sign In.
  3. If your account uses two-factor sign-in, the next screen asks for an Authentication code. Open your authenticator app and enter the 6-digit code, then click Verify. Lost your phone? Click Use a recovery code instead and enter one of the recovery codes you saved.

Depending on how your server is set up, you might also see:

  • Single sign-on buttons under or continue with. They sign you in with your organisation's account instead of an OnScreen password.
  • A Sign in with … link for a directory (LDAP) account. Click it to use your directory password; Use local account switches back.
  • A Forgot password? link next to the password label. It only appears when your admin has set up email. Enter your email address and click Send Reset Link; the email takes you to Choose a New Password.

Accepting an invitation

If your admin sent you an invite link, open it to reach Set Up Your Account. Choose a username and a password of at least 8 characters, click Create Account, then Sign In.

1.4Signing in on a TV with a code

Typing a password with a remote is slow, so most TV apps let you sign in from your phone or computer instead. This is called pairing.

  1. The first time you open the TV app, enter your server's address when it asks.
  2. On the sign-in screen, choose Sign in with another device. The TV shows an address ending in /pair and a 6-digit code.
  3. On your phone or computer, open that address. Sign in to the web app if it asks.
  4. Type the code into Pairing code. You can also give the TV a name under Device name (optional), such as "Living Room TV".
  5. Check the box headed You are about to authorise:, then click Authorise device. The TV signs in within a few seconds.

Only enter a code that is showing on a device you own. Whoever started that code gets access to your account, which is why the page warns you before you click Authorise device.

If your server uses single sign-on, pairing is how you sign in on a TV: you sign in on the web page with your organisation's account, and the TV follows. To type a username and password on the TV instead, choose Use password instead (LG, Samsung, Xbox, Roku) or simply stay on the Sign In screen (Android TV and Fire TV).

1.5Signing in on your phone

  1. Open the OnScreen phone app and type your server's address under Connect to your server. You don't need to type http:// or https://. Tap Continue.
  2. Sign in in one of three ways:
    • Type your Username and Password and tap Sign in with password.
    • If your server uses single sign-on, tap Sign in with …. The phone opens your server's sign-in page and signs in by itself when you finish.
    • Tap Pair this phone to get a code, then enter it on the /pair page from a computer, as in 1.4.
  3. If your account uses two-factor sign-in, enter the 6-digit code (or a recovery code) and tap Verify.

1.6Profiles and PINs

A household can share one account and still keep things apart with managed profiles. Each profile has its own watch history and progress, and can have its own 4-digit PIN and content-rating limit. Profiles are created by your server's admin.

Switching profile in the web app

  1. Click your name at the bottom of the sidebar. The Switch User panel lists your profiles.
  2. Click a profile and type its PIN under Enter 4-digit PIN. OnScreen switches as soon as the fourth digit is in and reloads the Home screen.
  • A profile marked No PIN set can't be switched to. Ask your admin to give it a PIN.
  • From a profile you can't switch straight to another one. Sign out and sign in with your account first.
  • Too many wrong PINs locks switching for a while ("too many failed attempts; try again later").
  • The TV and phone apps have no profile switcher; they use the account you signed in with.

Removing something from Continue Watching

Point at the tile and click the × that appears (Remove from Continue Watching). The title leaves the Continue Watching rows and Next Up until you watch it again. On a TV app, hold OK on the tile (the row says Hold OK for options); on the phone, press and hold the card.

Choosing and ordering your rows

  1. Click Customize rows at the top right of Home.
  2. In Hub rows, use ↑ and ↓ to move a row, and untick Show to hide it. The Libraries grid is in the list too.

Your layout is saved to your account, so the Android TV, Fire TV, phone, LG, Samsung and Xbox apps use the same order. It only affects you.

2.3Browsing a library

Open a library from the Libraries row on Home.

  • Filter… narrows the titles on the page by name.
  • Sort with Title, Year, Rating or Added. Click the highlighted one again to flip the order.
  • All Genres picks one genre.
  • The watch filter shows All items, Unwatched, In progress or Watched.
  • Surprise me opens a random title that matches your filters.
  • Browse genres and Browse years open lists by genre and by decade or year. Movie libraries add Browse studios; TV libraries add Browse networks.
  • Movie and TV libraries have a Collections tab with the film series and collections in that library. Music libraries have Artists and Albums tabs instead (chapter 4).

On each tile:

  • A check mark means watched, a number counts unwatched episodes, and a bar shows how far you've got.
  • The ⋯ menu has Mark watched / Mark unwatched.
  • The + button adds the title to a playlist (movie and TV libraries).

The TV apps offer the same sorting and filters: Sort (Title A–Z / Z–A, Recently added, Newest year, Oldest year, Highest rated), Genre, the watch filter and Surprise me.

2.4Title pages

Clicking a movie or show opens its page. (Clicking an episode starts it straight away.)

Buttons on a movie page

  • Play, or Resume · 0:23 if you've started it.
  • Play on… sends it to another of your devices (3.9).
  • Mark watched / Mark unwatched.
  • Download, if your admin allows downloads (chapter 8).
  • Report a problem (3.12).

Buttons on a show page

  • One play button that knows where you are: Resume S1 · E2, Play S3 · E5, Play S1 · E1 or Watch again.
  • Mark all watched and Mark all unwatched.
  • Request more seasons, when seasons are missing and you're allowed to request.
  • Season tabs (Season 1, Season 2, …) and the episode list. Each episode has its own watched toggle.

What else is on the page

  • Ratings: the year and content rating, then scores such as ★ (critics), 👥 (audience) and OnScreen (other users on your server). Where your admin has set it up, you'll also see IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes (RT) and Metacritic scores; the IMDb score links to IMDb.
  • Your rating: click a star to rate the title for yourself. Click your current star (or clear) to remove it.
  • Studio or network names link to a page of everything from that studio (2.7).
  • Chapters: for movies with chapter pictures, a strip of chapters. Click one to play from there.
  • Cast & Crew: click a person to see what else they're in.
  • Part of the …: other films in the same series, with See all. Films you don't have show Not in library.
  • Status: your watch status for the title (2.8).
  • For anime, links to AniList and MyAnimeList.

2.5Search

Click Search in the sidebar and start typing.

  • Results In your library can be narrowed with Movies, Shows, Episodes and Tracks. Movies and Shows are on to start with; tick Tracks to find music (artists and albums come with it). Your choice is remembered in that browser.
  • If some results are hidden by the boxes, a line tells you how many.
  • If your account can make requests, a second section, Ask your admin to add, lists titles that aren't on the server yet (2.9).

On the LG, Samsung and Xbox apps, Search has an on-screen keyboard and a Search in: choice of library. The Android TV and Google TV app also has voice search when the device supports it.

2.6Collections and film series

The Collections page groups things in four sections:

  • Collections made by your admin. A Smart collection fills itself from rules (for example "80s horror") and updates as the libraries change.
  • Playlists you've made. Click + New Playlist to start one (more in 4.6).
  • Film series such as the Alien or Toy Story films. A series appears once two of its films are on the server. Its page shows every film in the series; the ones you don't have are greyed out and may have a Request button.
  • Genres.

2.7People, studios and networks

  • People: click a face under Cast & Crew to see the person's photo, biography and everything of theirs on the server, grouped as Acting, Directing, Writing, Created and Produced.
  • Studios and networks NEW: click a studio or network name on a title page, or use Browse studios / Browse networks in a library. A studio page lists its titles and can be sorted by Title, Year, Rating or Date added.

2.8Favorites, watch status and history

Favorites

In the web app, click the heart at the top right of the player (Add to favorites). On the TV apps press ♥ Favorite (LG, Samsung, Xbox) or the heart (Android TV, Fire TV) on the title's page; on the phone, tap the heart on the title's page. Everything you've favorited is on the Favorites page.

Watch status

Movie and show pages have a Status list: Plan to Watch, Watching, On Hold, Completed or Dropped (— None — clears it). Titles set to Plan to Watch appear in the Plan to Watch row on Home. The phone app has the same choice as Watching status.

Watched and unwatched

OnScreen marks a title watched when you finish it. To change it yourself, use Mark watched / Mark unwatched on the title page, a tile's ⋯ menu, or the check mark at the top of the player. Shows and seasons have Mark all watched / Mark all unwatched. Marking something unwatched also clears its resume point.

History

History (Watch History) lists what you've played, with the app you used, how long and when. Entries can't be deleted.

2.9Asking for something new

If your server takes requests, you can ask your admin to get a movie or show. This works in the web app (and as Discover on the LG, Samsung and Xbox apps). The Android apps don't take requests.

  1. Search for the title. Under Ask your admin to add, click Request for admin to acquire for a movie, or Request for a show.
  2. For a show, choose the seasons in Choose seasons (or tick All seasons) and click the request button.
  3. Follow progress on the Requests page under My Requests. You can Cancel a request while it's pending, and Watch it once it's available.

Your account may have a limit, shown as for example "2 movie requests left this week". Over the limit, a request still goes in but needs the admin's approval. If your server is connected to download tools, the Upcoming tab shows a calendar of expected releases.

2.10Notifications

The bell at the bottom of the sidebar shows a count of unread notifications. Click it to see them; click one to open it, or Mark all read. You're told when:

  • a library scan adds new titles (Library scan complete) or a new title arrives;
  • a request of yours is submitted, approved, declined, fails to download, or becomes available (including Season N now available);
  • a problem you reported is resolved or closed.

3Watching

3.1Starting and resuming

  • Movies: press Play on the movie's page, or Resume · time to carry on.
  • Episodes start as soon as you open them, from where you left off.
  • Shows: the show page's play button resumes or starts the right episode.

To start a movie from the beginning in the web app, use the Chapters strip, drag the seek bar back, or mark the movie unwatched. On the Android TV, Fire TV, LG, Samsung and Xbox apps the title page has Play from Beginning.

Your position is saved as you watch, on every app. If you watch on another device while the web player is paused, the position and the Resume button update to match.

3.3Versions, editions and multi-part movies NEW

A title can have more than one version, for example a Director's Cut and the theatrical cut, or a 4K and a 1080p copy. When it does:

  • the web player has a Version menu, and the movie page shows which version is chosen;
  • the TV apps have a Version: button on the title page;
  • the phone shows Version choices on the title page.

Versions are named after the edition (such as "Director's Cut") or described, such as "4K · HDR10 · HEVC · MKV". A version marked DAMAGED (or "⚠ file may be damaged" on the page) failed the server's file check and may not play properly.

Multi-part movies (a film stored as two or more files, such as CD1 and CD2) play as one movie on every app, with one seek bar and one running time. The version menu shows them as "· 2 parts". They can't be cast.

Multi-episode files (one file holding two episodes) show as "E1–E2" or "S1 · E1–E2", count as both episodes, and are followed by the next episode after the range (E3).

3.4Audio tracks

When a file has more than one audio track (another language, or a commentary), the web player shows an Audio track button. Each track is listed by its name and language, with the channel count. OnScreen picks your preferred audio language automatically if one is set (9.6).

Switching to a track that isn't the file's default may restart the stream for a moment while the server prepares it. On the TV apps open Audio from the player's button row (press ↓) or press the yellow button (LG, Samsung) or Y (Xbox).

3.5Subtitles

Choosing subtitles

Click the Subtitles button. The list starts with Off, then the file's tracks (marked forced or SDH where that applies), then subtitles downloaded for the title (marked · online). If you've set a preferred subtitle language, OnScreen picks it for you.

Size, color and outline

With a text subtitle chosen, the menu adds Size (Small, Medium, Large), Color (White, Yellow, Black, Red), Background (None, Translucent, Opaque) and Outline (None, Light, Heavy). These are remembered in that browser. The phone app has the same choices under Subtitle style.

Subtitles out of sync

Use the Sync row: −1s and −0.1 show subtitles earlier, +0.1 and +1s later, and Reset puts them back. This lasts for the current viewing only. If a file is always out of step, use Report a problem ▸ Subtitles problem: an admin can fix the timing on the server, and the fix then applies in every app.

Finding subtitles online

If your server is connected to OpenSubtitles, the menu has Search online…. Choose a language, click Search, and pick a result; it downloads and is turned on. The Android TV and Google TV app (Find more online…), the phone app and the LG, Samsung and Xbox apps have the same search. Fire TV and Roku don't.

Picture-based subtitles (Blu-ray and DVD) NEW

Subtitles copied from Blu-rays and DVDs (PGS and VobSub) are pictures, not text, so a player can't simply draw them over the video. In v2.6, when the server is preparing the stream for you (rather than sending the file as it is), these tracks appear in the Subtitles menu like any other, in one of two ways:

  • "· text": the track has already been converted to text. Choosing it works like any other subtitle, with no restart.
  • "(burned in)": choosing it restarts the video at the same moment with the subtitles drawn into the picture. You'll see the loading circle briefly. Size, color and Sync don't apply to burned-in subtitles.

Choose Off (or a text track) to remove burned-in subtitles; the video restarts without them. Switching audio track or quality keeps the burned-in subtitles on. If a track can't be burned in (for example on a multi-part movie), a message says so and your video carries on.

This works on the web, Android TV, Fire TV, phone, LG, Samsung, Xbox and Roku apps. On Roku, picture-based subtitles are always burned in.

In the web player the menu may also offer Convert … to text (OCR), which turns a picture-based track into text so it can be styled and synced. It takes a few minutes; the text track is turned on when it's ready. If OCR isn't installed on the server, it says so.

3.6Skip Intro, Credits and Ad

When an episode's intro or end credits are marked on the server, a button appears while they play: Skip Intro → or Skip Credits →. Click it, or press S.

  • During an intro, Always skip intros turns on automatic skipping in that browser. Each intro is skipped once, so you can seek back into it to watch it.
  • Recordings from live TV can show Skip Ad → during a detected ad break, with Always skip ads (7.3).
  • On TV apps, the skip button is selected for you: press OK (LG, Samsung and Xbox show Press OK to skip Intro). Back dismisses it. On Roku press ★.

3.8Quality and direct play

When your device can play a file exactly as it is stored, OnScreen sends it unchanged. This is called direct play and gives the best picture. When it can't (an unusual format, a slow connection, or a limit on your account), the server converts the video as you watch.

The Quality menu (web)

The Quality button offers Auto (Direct Play), 4K (2160p), 1080p, 720p, 480p and 360p. Choices higher than the file itself are left out, and Auto (Direct Play) is left out when the file can't be played as it is. Pick a lower setting if playback keeps pausing to load.

Playback quality for your account NEW

To set limits for every device at once, use Account ▸ Playback quality in the web app, or Settings ▸ Playback quality in the Android TV, Fire TV, phone, LG, Samsung and Xbox apps. Details are in 9.2. The TV apps have no quality menu in the player; this setting is how you choose quality there.

Your admin can also set a bitrate limit for watching from outside your home network. Anything above it is converted too, so playback away from home may look softer than at home.

3.9Play on another device and casting

Play on…

Send what you're watching in the web app to one of your other devices, such as your TV:

  1. Open OnScreen on the TV first, so the server has seen it recently.
  2. In the web app, click Play on… on the movie page, or the Play on another device icon in the player.
  3. Pick the device. The web player pauses and the TV carries on from the same moment.

The list shows your own devices that have played something in the last 30 days. The LG, Samsung, Xbox, Android TV and Fire TV apps can receive. The phone and Roku can't.

Chromecast

  • Web: in Chrome or Edge, a Cast to TV button appears for files that can play directly on a Chromecast (MP4, M4V, MOV or WebM with H.264 or HEVC video). Casting starts where you are and pauses the browser.
  • Phone: the player has a Cast button. It sends the original file (H.264 or HEVC video in MP4, M4V, MOV or WebM, and common music formats). Subtitles aren't sent.

Casting sends the original file, so a video above your playback quality limit can't be cast. Multi-part movies can't be cast either.

3.10Sleep timer

The moon icon in the web player stops playback after 15 minutes, 30 minutes, 45 minutes, 1 hour or at the End of episode. Off cancels it. The music player has the same timer, with End of track (or End of chapter for audiobooks). The phone app has a sleep timer too.

3.11Messages you might see

4Music

4.1Browsing music

A music library has two tabs, Artists and Albums. Sort artists by Title, Year, Rating or Added, and albums by Title, Artist, Year or Added. All Genres, Browse genres and Browse years narrow the albums. Music videos appear on a shelf at the top of the Artists tab.

To find a particular song, use Search with Tracks ticked.

4.2Artists and albums

An artist page has these sections:

  • Albums, newest first.
  • Appears on NEW: albums by others that the artist features on, with the matching tracks.
  • Similar artists NEW: artists on your server with a similar sound.
  • Music Videos.

Instant mix NEW plays a mix of the artist and similar music on your server, replacing what's playing. It's also on album pages and in a track's details. The TV apps have Instant mix too, plus Play All and Shuffle on artist pages.

An album page shows the year, track count and length, with ▶ Play and Instant mix. Albums on more than one disc are split under Disc 1, Disc 2 and so on. Click a track number to play from that track.

4.3The music player

Music plays in a bar at the bottom of the web app, so you can keep browsing. From left to right:

  • × (Close player) stops and clears what's playing.
  • The cover, title and artist (links to the artist and album).
  • Shuffle, Previous, Play/Pause, Next and Repeat (off → all → one). Previous restarts the track if you're more than 3 seconds in.
  • The seek bar, then the sleep timer, RG (ReplayGain, 4.5) and volume.

On the phone and Android TV, music keeps playing after you leave the app, with controls on the lock screen and in the notification area (phone) or the system's media controls (TV).

4.4Track details, lyrics and quality

Each track on an album page has a quality badge: Hi-Res (lossless, better than CD) or Lossless. Point at a track and click ⓘ (File details) to see:

  • the format, bit depth, sample rate, channels and bitrate;
  • ReplayGain values, and whether they came from the file's tags or the server measured them;
  • Lyrics, when the server has them;
  • MusicBrainz links for the recording, release and artist.

The phone app shows lyrics from a Lyrics button in the player.

4.5ReplayGain and gapless playback

ReplayGain evens out loudness so one track doesn't blast after a quiet one. It's off unless you turn it on, and it's set per browser or device.

  • Click RG in the music player, or open Audio in the sidebar.
  • Choose Off, Track (each track at the same loudness) or Album (whole albums at the same loudness, keeping the differences between their tracks).
  • Preamp raises or lowers everything by up to 15 dB. The gain never pushes a track past its peak, so it doesn't distort.

Tracks without ReplayGain tags play at their own level, unless your server has measured them (shown as Analyzed in the track's details). The phone app has the same choice in Settings ▸ ReplayGain.

Gapless playback: the web player loads the next track before the current one ends, so live albums and continuous mixes play without a gap. The phone plays queues gaplessly too.

The OnScreen desktop app adds a native audio engine for bit-perfect playback, with its options on the Audio page. The web music player always plays the original files.

4.6Playlists and M3U files

Making a playlist

  • On the Collections page, click + New Playlist.
  • Add a movie or episode with the + button (Add to playlist) in the player or on a library tile, then choose a playlist or + New Playlist.
  • Your playlists are listed under Playlists on the Collections page; open one to edit it or remove items.

Importing and exporting M3U playlists NEW

Import and export live on the My Playlists page: click Playlists in the sidebar.

  1. Click Import playlist and choose an .m3u or .m3u8 file (up to 1 MB) exported from another player.
  2. Give it a name (or leave the file name) and click Import.
  3. OnScreen matches each line to something on the server by path, folder and file name, or artist and title, and says how many it matched. Lines it couldn't match are listed. Web addresses in the file are never opened.

To export, open a playlist there and click Export .m3u8. Tick Full server paths if the other player needs the files' full paths on the server.

The My Playlists page also makes smart playlists (tick Smart playlist (rule-based)), which fill themselves from rules such as type, genre, year and rating.

5Audiobooks, podcasts and books

5.1Audiobooks

Audiobook libraries are organised by author and series. A book's page shows its chapters and a play button:

  • ▶ Play for a new book, or ▶ Resume · ch 3 · 12:40 to carry on. You resume at the start of the chapter you were in.
  • Start over goes back to chapter 1.
  • Click a chapter to play from it.

Audiobooks play in the music player bar, which adds two buttons:

  • Speed (shows e.g. 1×): 0.75× to 3×. The voice keeps its normal pitch, and the speed is saved for that book on the server, so it follows you to other devices.
  • Add bookmark: saves the current moment, with an optional note.

Bookmarks are listed on the book's page under Bookmarks. Click a bookmark's time to play from there, or use Add note / Edit note and Delete. The sleep timer can stop at the End of chapter.

Other apps: the phone has speed, bookmarks and a sleep timer. Android TV, Fire TV, LG, Samsung and Xbox have speed (on LG, Samsung and Xbox press ↓ during an audiobook). Roku plays audiobooks at normal speed only.

5.2Podcasts

Podcast libraries list shows; open one to see its Episodes, newest first, with the date and length. Click an episode to play it. Your admin chooses which podcasts the server follows and how many episodes it keeps; new episodes download automatically. To suggest a podcast, ask your admin.

5.3Reading books and comics

OnScreen reads EPUB books and CBZ and CBR comics in the web app and the phone app. Open a book to start reading.

Turning pages

  • Click ‹ or › at the sides, or drag the slider at the bottom.
  • Keys: ← →, PgUp PgDn or Space to turn pages, Home and End to jump to the first or last page.
  • EPUB books show Section 3 of 144 at the top; comics show Page 1 of 36.

Comic options

  • Reading direction: Left → Right, Right → Left (manga) or Vertical (webtoon). Manga usually opens right-to-left by itself. The arrow keys follow the direction.
  • Layout: Single page, Two-page spread or Long scroll.

Picking up where you left off NEW

Your page is saved to the server as you read. If you've read further on another device, the web reader offers You're further along on … with Continue from page 40 on … or Stay here. This works for EPUB, CBZ and CBR in the web app; the phone app syncs comic pages (CBZ and CBR).

The TV apps don't read books ("Book reading isn't available on TV. Open this book in the web or phone app.").

5.4Reading in an e-reader app (OPDS) NEW

E-reader apps that support OPDS catalogs, such as KOReader, Moon+ Reader, Panels, Chunky and Thorium, can browse, search and download your books.

  1. Make a key. In the web app go to Account ▸ API keys ▸ Create API key. Name it after the device (for example "Kobo"), set Access to Read-only (GET requests only), and click Create key. Copy the key (it starts with osk_); it's shown only once.
  2. Add the catalog in the app. The address is shown on the Account page under Read your books in an e-reader app (with a Copy button). It's your server's address followed by /opds/.
  3. Sign in with the key. Enter anything as the user name (your username is fine) and paste the key as the password.
  • KOReader: Search ▸ OPDS catalog ▸ +.
  • Moon+ Reader: Net Library ▸ Add.
  • Panels / Chunky: add an OPDS source with the address, then the user name and key.

The catalog shows Recently added, one entry per book library you can open, and search. You see exactly the books you'd see in the web app.

Your account password doesn't work here, on purpose. If a device is lost, revoke its key in Account ▸ API keys. The catalog needs downloads to be allowed on the server; if they're not, the Account page says so.

8Downloads

Downloads let you keep a copy of a movie or episode to watch without a connection. They only appear when your admin allows downloads. Otherwise the button is hidden, or you'll see "Downloads are turned off on this server."

8.1Downloading in the web app

  1. On a movie or episode page, click Download.
  2. Choose a size:
    • Original: the file exactly as it's stored ("the file as it is"). It starts straight away.
    • A smaller copy NEW: 1080p · 8 Mbps, 720p · 4 Mbps, 720p · 2 Mbps or 480p · 1 Mbps. These are MP4 files the server prepares first.
  3. For a smaller copy, the button shows Waiting… and then Preparing 40%. The download starts by itself when the copy is ready. You can leave the page; the server keeps working.
  • Sizes above your account's limit are greyed out and marked "above your limit".
  • If a file can't be converted, the menu says No smaller copies for this file.
  • "You already have downloads being prepared" means wait for one of them to finish before asking for another.

8.2Your downloads list

Account ▸ Downloads lists the smaller copies you've asked for, with their size and status: Waiting to start, Preparing · N%, Ready, Failed or Expired.

  • Download saves a ready copy again; Cancel stops one being prepared; Remove deletes it from the list.
  • The server keeps each copy for a while after you last downloaded it (a week unless your admin changed it), then removes it to save space. An expired copy can simply be asked for again.

8.3Downloads on your phone

The phone app keeps downloads inside the app for watching offline.

  1. On a movie, episode, track or book page, tap Download.
  2. For video, choose Original or a smaller copy (the same sizes as the web). If nothing smaller is offered, the original downloads straight away.
  3. The button shows the progress (Queued, Preparing N%, then a percentage) and finally Downloaded. Tap Downloaded to remove it.
  • All your downloads are under ⋮ ▸ Downloads, with the space they use. Tap one to play it; the trash icon removes it.
  • If the phone has no connection when you open the app, it goes straight to Downloads and shows Offline — showing your downloaded items.
  • Settings ▸ Download only over Wi-Fi (on by default) waits for Wi-Fi before downloading.

Signing out of the phone app, or choosing Forget server, deletes every download on the phone.

The TV apps don't download.

11Troubleshooting and FAQ

The video keeps stopping to load (buffering).

  • Choose a lower setting in the web player's Quality menu, or lower Playback quality for your account (it's the only quality control in the TV apps).
  • Watching away from home? Your admin may have a lower limit for viewers outside the home network.
  • If the web player says "Playback keeps stalling", the file may be damaged or the server busy. Report it.

A title won't play.

  • Read the message (3.11). "Too many streams" means stop playback on another device first.
  • "Dolby Vision is not supported" or "This file appears to be damaged": ask your admin for another copy, using Report a problem.
  • Try another app or a browser. If it fails everywhere, report it.

Subtitles are out of sync.

In the web player, use the Sync row in the Subtitles menu (3.5). It only lasts for that viewing, so also send Report a problem ▸ Subtitles problem: an admin can save the right timing on the server for every app. Or try a different track, or Search online… for another one.

I can't see the subtitles I chose.

  • Picture-based (Blu-ray/DVD) tracks marked "(burned in)" take a moment to appear, because the video restarts.
  • On Roku, only subtitles inside the video file are offered.
  • On Fire TV, the Subtitles button only appears when the title has a subtitle track.

I was signed out.

  • Someone may have signed the device out from Signed-in devices, or your admin may have. Sign in again. If you didn't do it and don't know who did, ask your admin to change your password.
  • A device that hasn't been used for a long time has to sign in again.

The TV pairing code doesn't work.

  • Make sure you're signed in on the /pair page with the right account, and you typed all 6 digits.
  • Codes expire. The TV shows a new one by itself (Android TV says "The previous code expired — enter the NEW code shown above"); type the new code.
  • If the TV says it can't reach the server, check the server address on the TV (Change server).
  • Still stuck? Choose Use password instead, or use the Sign In screen.

Something is missing from my library.

Your admin decides which libraries you can open, and may have a content-rating limit or label restrictions on your account. If you can make requests, search for the title and ask for it (2.9).

I can't find the Download button.

Downloads are off on your server. Ask your admin. The same setting controls the e-reader catalog and photo Download original.

Can I watch on two devices at once?

Usually yes, up to your account's stream limit. Opening the same title on a second device takes over from the first.

Can I cast to a Chromecast?

From the web app in Chrome or Edge, or from the phone app, when the file can play directly on the Chromecast and is within your Playback quality limit (3.9). Otherwise use Play on… to send it to an OnScreen TV app.

My music is louder or quieter from track to track.

Turn on ReplayGain (4.5). It's set separately in each browser and on the phone.

Where are the settings for my profile's PIN or rating limit?

Your admin manages those.

How do I change my password?

On the Account page, under Password (9.6). If you've forgotten it, use Forgot password? on the sign-in page if it's there, or ask your admin.
